Dune: Awakening / PvP in the Deep Desert00 / Introduction
Setup00

Introduction

Right now the Deep Desert PvP instance rests on three problems:

  1. No goal. The harvesting multiplier does not offset the risk of losing a vehicle, cargo and time.
  2. No structure. Players roam the map looking for random fights that quickly stop being fun.
  3. No progression. PvP gives nothing that cannot be obtained in PvE.

The game already has a ready political cycle: the weekly Landsraad Term, the Landsraad Board with House requirements, a vote and a Decree for the following week that applies to the whole server. Control of the Board grants a Sysselraad, a line of completed requirements horizontally, vertically or diagonally, and the right to choose the Decree.

The proposal plugs the Deep Desert into that cycle. Guilds fight not for abstract points but for cells on the Landsraad Board. A captured concession completes a House requirement, a line of concessions gives a Sysselraad, the Sysselraad gives a Decree, and the Decree changes the rules of the Deep Desert for a week. No mechanic is introduced from scratch: it uses the existing Board, Term, vote, Decrees, House requirements, Research menu, Fabricator, augments and consumables.

What the proposal builds on

  • The Landsraad Board with Houses and requirements completed by player actions.
  • A weekly Term, a vote and a Decree that applies to the whole server.
  • The Sysselraad as a line of completed requirements that grants control of the Board.
  • Guilds aligned with the Atreides or the Harkonnen, competing for the favour of the Landsraad.
  • Crafting at the Fabricator, base building on sub-fiefs in the Deep Desert and the Research menu for regular schematics.
  • Deep Desert resources, including titanium and Stravidium Mass for Plastanium.
  • Augments, consumables and cosmetics as existing item categories.
Dune: Awakening / PvP in the Deep Desert01 / Territorial control
Concessions01

1. Territorial control: concessions and mandates

How it works. The Deep Desert map holds 15 imperial concessions: one per each of the 9 shards and 6 more in the central and northern sectors. They are abandoned imperial stations, old mines and sietch ruins. Each concession is tied to a specific House on the Landsraad Board and to a tile number. The concession UI shows which requirement it completes.

  • Capture goes through the concession terminal, a 30 second channel. Any damage taken interrupts it.
  • A concession has an integrity pool, for example 10,000 HP. Breaking it takes time and coordinated action, not one player.
  • Holding gives two buffs. Economic: +5% spice harvesting on the whole tile, about 2.67 by 2.67 km. Tactical: +3% shield damage within 500 m of the terminal.
  • A line of three concessions in neighbouring tiles gives +10% spice in those tiles while the line holds.
  • Holding also gives credit toward a House requirement. Credit grows over time and then decays, so holding one point forever is not worthwhile.
  • Holding limit: 3 concessions per guild.
  • Ownership resets at the end of the Term, along with the mandate review. The map does not freeze, and the cycle matches the Landsraad rhythm.

Sectors

The Deep Desert is a 9 by 9 grid, 81 tiles, about 24 by 24 km and more than 500 square km. The first line stands on the Shield Wall, entry is from the south, players push north, and that is used as a risk gradient:

  • Southern sectors, rows A-B, 18 tiles: cold zone. No concessions, few resources, low risk. Starter area for newcomers and farmers.
  • Central sectors, rows C-F, 36 tiles: warm zone. 6 concessions, about one per 6 tiles, moderate rewards, medium risk.
  • Northern sectors, rows G-I, 27 tiles: hot zone. 9 concessions, one per 3 tiles, top rewards, highest PvP density. Artifacts from section 5 also appear here.

In hot zones a concession gives double credit toward a House requirement, but the economic spice buff does not apply: the north pays in politics, not in economy. Holding the north is profitable but expensive in gear.

What the player gets. A clear reason to fight for a specific mine: it moves the Board, not just a guild counter.

Dune: Awakening / PvP in the Deep Desert02 / Season
Cycle02

2. Season: four Terms and the Grand Landsraad

How it works. A season lasts 30 days and splits into four week-long Terms. Season score comes from captured Sysselraads and held concessions. Kills and destroyed vehicles do not move the season on their own, they complete House requirements.

  • After the fourth Term comes the Grand Landsraad: a two-hour server event.
  • The winning faction is the one that held the Board for more Terms, not the one with the most kills.
  • The title "Governor of Arrakis" is confirmed at the Grand Landsraad and ratified by the Emperor. The Landsraad chooses, House Corrino confirms. An Imperial refusal can be an event twist, but not a cancellation of the result.
  • The title goes to the guild leader, shows in the profile and unlocks unique cosmetics.
  • "Warden of the Desert" is renamed to "Concession Mandatary". It goes to the guild that held a line of concessions the longest and grants +10% spice yield in the Deep Desert while the line holds.
  • Everyone who reaches the minimum credit threshold gets a chest with scrip and a rare resource.
  • The trailing faction gets an audit discount: its House requirements close 10% faster during the next Term.

What we remove. The separate 30-day campaign currency. Board influence is the season score, duplicating it adds nothing.

Dune: Awakening / PvP in the Deep Desert03 / PvP economy
Economy03

3. PvP economy: schematics, components and new fabricators

How it works. The currency is Landsraad scrip. It is earned only through Deep Desert actions:

  • killing an agent of a hostile House: 10-20 scrip;
  • capturing a concession: 50;
  • destroying a rival's vehicle: 100;
  • completing a PvP House requirement: 200-300.

Scrip is spent at the Landsraad Emissary at the outpost. He sells imperial warrants, consumables and cosmetics. PvP components are not sold, they are found in the desert. Regular schematics stay in the Research menu.

  1. Imperial warrants. A warrant is a normal PvP schematic. It is bought for scrip, learned like any other schematic and opens a recipe for a blade, carbine, heavy weapon or armour. Price 500-1500 scrip depending on tier.
  2. PvP components. New items found only in the Deep Desert: "Imperial Blank" (body and barrel), "Warrant Seal" (gives the item imperial status) and "Spice Bond" (carries PvP stats). There are no recipes: find it, take it, bring it to the fabricator.
  3. New fabricators. PvP weapons, armour and gear are assembled at new PvP fabricators that can only be built in the Deep Desert, on a sub-fief. There is no access to such fabricators in safe zones, so PvP items cannot be crafted outside the desert.
  4. PvP augments, three of them, in the same slots as regular augments: "Shield Piercer" (damage to player shields, penalty against NPCs), "Duelist" (damage to players, penalty against NPCs), "Ambush Master" (stealth bonus against players only).
  5. Consumables and cosmetics. "Adrenaline Surge" stimulant, "Spice Fever" drink, "Sand Veil" grenade. Cloaks, helmets and vehicle paints with House colours or the "Governor of Arrakis" title.

Assembly only in the desert

New fabricators turn a temporary base into a strategic point: they have to be placed, supplied, defended and used to craft gear before the Term resets. A base in the desert becomes a production site, not just a storage box.

Desert-only restriction

  • A finished item gets the "Heart of the Desert" tag and can only be equipped in the Deep Desert.
  • Outside the desert it cannot be equipped and does not take part in PvE.
  • Destroying a fabricator does not cancel a learned schematic, but components have to be found again.

Economy limits

  • Scrip from killing the same player: no more than 3 times a day, with diminishing returns.
  • Once a week before the vote there is a sealed auction: rare warrants and dossiers on rivals.
  • PvP fabricators are built only in the Deep Desert. They do not exist in safe zones.

What the player gets. A chain: fought, earned scrip, bought a warrant, found components, built a PvP fabricator in the desert, crafted gear, returned to the fight. Every step relies on existing mechanics: base building, fabricators, loot and schematics.

Dune: Awakening / PvP in the Deep Desert04 / Contracts
Quests04

4. Contracts: Landsraad quests

How it works. Contracts are a PvP branch of House requirements on the Landsraad Board, completed only in the Deep Desert.

Examples:

  • destroy 3 vehicles of a rival House;
  • extract 500 spice without losses;
  • hold concession X for ten minutes;
  • kill 10 agents of a hostile House in sector Y.
  • Completion gives cell credit and scrip, about twice a regular requirement because the risk is higher.
  • Thresholds scale with population: lower at low activity, higher at high activity. Same logic as existing House requirements.
  • A group requirement for 3+ players counts for the whole group, but only if everyone is alive at turn-in.
  • Requirements in hot zones G-I give 20% more credit.

What the player gets. Understanding that the trip is not just farming, but a contribution to a Decree that will change server rules in two days.

Dune: Awakening / PvP in the Deep Desert05 / Artifacts
Artifacts05

5. Artifacts: three equal goals

How it works. The Deep Desert has three artifacts. They are equal in weight: each gives its own benefit, and none is an appendix to the others.

  • Opafire. A rare opaline stone from Hagal, shimmering in all colours. Found in buried treasure chests on open sand, chests appear randomly in regions with large sandy spaces between solid ground. Role: money and yield. Delivery gives scrip and +5% concession yield for a day.
  • Ship Manifest. A component with data on cargo, crew and ship routes. It shows trade routes and spice shipments. Recovered from ship wrecks that randomly fall in open desert areas of the Deep Desert. Role: intel and audits. Delivery gives a dossier with routes and shipments: a rival loses 20% point yield for 24 hours and its House requirements close more slowly.
  • Shai-Hulud tooth. A trophy left only after a player dies in a worm's mouth. The worm is immortal, so there is no other way to get the tooth. (With this mechanic, the tooth drop on PvE maps must be removed.) The tooth cannot be stored in a bank or container, it disappears when the carrier dies, and one guild can hold only one tooth. Role: combat power. While carried, the carrier and escort get +10% damage against players. Delivery gives the guild +10% damage against players in the Deep Desert for a week.

Delivery

  • Any of the three artifacts appears no more than once a day per server. For the tooth this means only one tooth can drop per server per day.
  • The carrier cannot use a vehicle other than a sandbike or a light ground vehicle, cannot raise a Holtzman shield, and is marked on the map for everyone in the Deep Desert.
  • Objective: deliver the cargo to a base or to the Emissary.
  • Every escort member gets scrip and a chance at unique cosmetics.
  • The artifact can be sold to a vendor for solaris, but then the guild loses the buff and the dossier.

Flight restriction

In hot zones a ceiling is introduced: while an artifact is active, ornithopters within one kilometre receive an "Imperial patrol" warning and cannot climb above 50 metres, losing speed. The Landsraad has closed the airspace for an investigation.

Sandworms remain a natural limiter. Without a shield and on the ground, the group has to coordinate: one distracts the worm, another carries the cargo.

Dune: Awakening / PvP in the Deep Desert06 / Politics
Influence06

6. Political actions of guilds

Between Terms, guilds spend accumulated influence on political actions.

  • Resolution. A guild spends influence on a resolution: open a new concession in a sector, raise the spice quota, declare an Imperial bounty on a player or guild, or request an audit of a rival. Resolutions are filed between Terms, not during the vote.
  • Lobbying. Before the vote, guilds put scrip into a House so it supports a Decree draft. The House takes the deposit but is not obliged to deliver: if the draft fails, the scrip burns. It is a risk, not a guarantee.
  • Imperial bounty. A map marker for everyone, a reward for the kill from the Landsraad fund. Expires in a week or can be bought out.
  • Audit. A yield inspection: 20% less harvesting for 24 hours, the difference goes to the trailing faction's fund. Triggered by a Manifest dossier or by a resolution.
  • Proxy war. A guild formally fights for a House, not for itself. The patron has interests, rewards and grudges against rivals.
  • Espionage. A report shows which concessions a rival holds and how much cell credit it has. Lasts 12 hours, costs a lot.

What the player gets. The politics the game lacks between Terms: not only fighting, but investing, risking, framing a rival and buying your way out of mistakes.

Dune: Awakening / PvP in the Deep Desert07 / Decrees
Decrees07

7. Landsraad Decrees and the Deep Desert

Existing Decrees already change the number of sub-fiefs for bases, security zones and spice output. They should extend to the Deep Desert, and three new drafts should join the same pool:

  • "Open Concessions": +20% spice yield across the Deep Desert, +1 concession limit per guild, Holtzman shields do not work within concession radius.
  • "Imperial Oversight": player damage outside hot zones reduced by 50%, but kills in G-I grant double credit. A Decree for servers that want controlled PvP.
  • "Spice Quota": Emissary prices rise by 25%, Opafire delivery gives double scrip and partially clears a bounty.

Existing Decrees should also be felt in the Deep Desert:

  • "Spice Increase" raises concession income, "Spice Decrease" cuts it.
  • "Security Zone Increase" expands the safe southern sectors, "Security Zone Decrease" shrinks them and opens the centre to PvP.
  • "Fiefdom Increase" gives a guild an extra sub-fief for a base, "Fiefdom Decrease" removes a plot or raises the weekly land tax.

What the player gets. Every Landsraad vote shows up on the map they walk on, not just on a faction screen.

Dune: Awakening / PvP in the Deep Desert08 / Legal standing
Imperial peace08

8. Legal standing instead of reputation

How it works. A separate reputation system is unnecessary. There is an Imperial peace and there are violations of it.

  • Killing your own and neutrals raises the wanted level. Emissary prices rise, access to law-abiding House requirements closes.
  • At a high wanted level a guild cannot file resolutions or earn scrip until the debt is cleared.
  • Recovery: Landsraad quests, a buyout, handing in a Manifest that documents someone else's violation.
  • Collusive kills are caught by audits. If two guilds keep killing each other without losing ground, the Landsraad cuts both their yields for a day.
Dune: Awakening / PvP in the Deep Desert09 / Weekly rhythm
Rhythm09

9. Weekly rhythm and statistics

  • Landsraad Session. Two hours before the vote, all influence and credit count double. Everyone has a reason to log in, including casual players.
  • Vote and Decree announcement. A server event broadcast in the UI. The Decree must be visible to everyone, otherwise nobody notices that the world changed.
  • House standings instead of a leaderboard. Guild score plus each player's personal contribution: concessions, kills, vehicles, delivered artifacts.
  • Session minutes. Who tabled a draft, who lobbied, who lost and how much they spent. Ready material for server politics and post-Term reviews.
Dune: Awakening / PvP in the Deep Desert10 / Risks
Balance10

10. Risks and safeguards

  • One guild dominating. Handled by audits, concession yield decay and ownership resets every Term.
  • Runaway influence. Handled by scrip burning at auctions and in lobbying, and a holding limit that does not grow forever.
  • Complexity for newcomers. Handled by keeping the south A-B free of concessions and politics: a new player only sees basic House requirements.
  • Toxicity. Handled by bounties, penalties for killing your own and audits of collusive kills.
  • PvE devaluation. Handled by making PvP gear unequippable outside the Deep Desert, with materials gathered only there.
  • UI overload. Handled by keeping every element on the existing Landsraad Board, with no separate screens.
Dune: Awakening / PvP in the Deep Desert11 / Conclusion
Conclusion11

Conclusion

The Deep Desert stops being a separate mode and becomes the field where guilds earn influence for the Houses of the Landsraad. Concessions are tied to Board cells, credit goes into House requirements, a line of completed requirements gives a Sysselraad, the Sysselraad gives a Decree, and the Decree changes Deep Desert rules for a week. Scrip, quests, artifacts and legal standing all feed that cycle instead of running alongside it.

PvE remains untouched: no PvE recipe is cut, no PvE resource is moved into the Deep Desert. A player who does not want PvP loses political offices, not access to content.

Everything above is a proposal for discussion and a set of hypothetical examples.
